Giant Panda

Green Sea Turtle

Green turtles migrate thousands of kilometers between feeding grounds and the exact beaches where they hatched. Nesting sites lost to coastal development, plastic ingestion, and bycatch in fishing gear are the biggest ongoing risks — protected nesting beaches have helped several populations recover.

Black Rhino

Hunted toward near-extinction for horn believed — wrongly — to have medicinal value, black rhino numbers fell more than 96% between 1970 and 1993. Intensive protection in fenced sanctuaries has since more than doubled the population, though poaching pressure never fully disappears.

Why species are disappearing

Five drivers, one pattern
01

Habitat loss

Forests, wetlands, and grasslands converted to farmland, roads, and cities remove the space species need to feed, breed, and migrate.

02

Poaching & illegal trade

Demand for ivory, horn, skins, and traditional medicine sustains a black market that outpaces enforcement in many range countries.

03

Climate disruption

Shifting temperatures and rainfall change nesting seasons, food availability, and habitable range faster than species can adapt.

04

Ocean & plastic pollution

Marine species ingest or become entangled in plastic debris and fishing gear, while runoff degrades coastal breeding grounds.

05

Human–wildlife conflict

As ranges shrink and overlap with farmland, animals and communities compete for the same land, often with lethal outcomes for wildlife.

What actually helps

Small, repeatable actions
→

Support verified conservation groups

Organizations like WWF, IUCN, and regional wildlife trusts fund the anti-poaching patrols and habitat programs behind most recovery stories on this page.

→

Choose sustainable seafood & palm oil

Fishing bycatch and palm plantations are two of the biggest habitat pressures on turtles, tigers, and elephants — your grocery choices reach further than you'd think.

→

Never buy wildlife products

Ivory, rhino horn, tiger bone, and exotic pet trade all trace back to a live animal removed from a shrinking population.

→

Reduce single-use plastic

Sea turtles and countless marine species mistake plastic for food. Cutting personal plastic waste is one of the most direct actions available to anyone.
